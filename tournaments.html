Before You Play

Rules to Know

✗

Illegal Moves

An illegal move is any action that violates the basic movement laws of chess. Common examples include:

  • Moving Incorrectly: Moving a piece in a way it can’t move (e.g., moving a bishop off its diagonal).
  • Jumping Pieces: Jumping over pieces with something other than a knight.
  • Invalid Castling: Making an invalid castling attempt.
  • Completing a Move: A move is only considered “completed” once you have released the piece and pressed the clock.
  • Correcting a Move: If you realize you made an illegal move before pressing the clock, you can change it—though you are still bound by the Touch-Move rule.
✋

Touch Move

Once a round begins, you must be very deliberate with your physical actions:

  • Your Pieces: If you intentionally touch one of your own pieces, you must move it, provided it has a legal move. If it has no legal moves, you can make any other legal move without penalty.
  • Opponent’s Pieces: If you intentionally touch an opponent’s piece, you must capture it if it is legally possible to do so.
  • Adjusting Pieces: If a piece is off-centre on its square and you want to straighten it without being forced to move it, you must clearly announce “I adjust” or “J’adoube” before touching it. You can only do this on your own turn.
  • Castling: When castling, you must touch your King first, then your Rook. Touching the Rook first may legally obligate you to only move the Rook.
⏱

Ending Your Turn

Managing the chess clock is a fundamental part of tournament play.

  • The One-Hand Rule: You must complete your move on the board and press your clock using the same hand. Moving a piece with your left hand and tapping the clock with your right hand is strictly forbidden.
  • Finality: Once you take your hand off a piece after making a legal move, you cannot change your mind and move it to a different square. Your turn officially ends the moment you press the clock.
½

Claiming a Draw

Apart from checkmate or running out of time, games can end in a tie (a draw), split equally at 0.5 points each. You can claim or offer a draw under specific conditions:

  • Stalemate & Dead Position: Automatic draws. Stalemate occurs when a player has no legal moves and their King is not in check. A dead position occurs when neither player has enough material to legally force a checkmate (e.g., King vs. King).
  • Threefold Repetition: You can claim a draw if the exact same board position occurs three times in a game.
  • 50-Move Rule: You can claim a draw if 50 consecutive moves have been played by both sides without a single pawn moving or any piece being captured.
  • Mutual Agreement: You can verbally offer a draw to your opponent. Correct etiquette is to make your move, verbally state “I offer a draw,” and then press your clock. Do not repeatedly spam offers, as this is penalized as harassment.
✎

Recording of Moves (Chess Notation)

In standard tournament play, players are required to write down every move made during the game using Algebraic Notation (e.g., 1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6).

  • The Scoresheet: You must write down your move and your opponent’s move on an official scoresheet after the move is played.
  • Why it matters: Keeping an accurate scoresheet is the only way you can legally prove a claim to the Tournament Director, such as a draw by Threefold Repetition or the 50-Move Rule.
  • Note: In CFC / FIDE rated Blitz or Rapid games the recording of moves is encouraged but not mandatory. The recording of moves is mandatory in CFC and FIDE classical time controls as well as any time control that has a 30 or more second increment bonus per move.

⚑

Penalties

If you violate a rule, do not argue with your opponent. Stop the chess clock immediately to pause the game time, raise your hand, and summon the arbiter or Tournament Director.

  • First Illegal Move: The arbiter will reset the board to the position before the illegal move. As a penalty, extra time (2 minutes in standard time controls, and 1 minute in rapid / blitz time controls) will be added to your opponent’s clock.
  • Second Illegal Move: Making a second illegal move in the exact same game results in an automatic forfeit and loss of the game.
  • Conduct Penalties: Talking to your opponent during the game, distracting them, or bringing electronic devices (like cell phones) into the playing hall can result in time penalties, warnings, or immediate game forfeiture.
